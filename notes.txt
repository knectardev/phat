## What this pivot actually means

You're not adding a second layer to the existing PHAT demo. You're **replacing** the current matrix (Effort × Result, with the Wyckoff laws and named transitions built on top of it) with a new matrix whose axes are **Executed Volume × L2 Depth**. That's a different conceptual model — one where the coordinates tell you about *execution vs. resting intent*, not *effort vs. result*.

Everything built on the old axes becomes irrelevant or needs re-derivation:
- Quadrant names: Vacuum/Engine/Apathy/Wall → new names like Slip/Engine/Coherence/Hidden Floor
- Named vector transitions: Fake-out/Confirmation/Climax/Exhaustion/Ignition/Thinning are all defined against the old axes and don't port over
- The five reversal laws (Fat Reversal, Vacuum Snap, Curvature, Divergence, Liquidity Divergence) were tuned for Effort×Result and would need to be rewritten for Volume×Depth
- The Heikin-Ashi regime filter, HMA smoothing, and trend-persistent bias — these apply to any candle series, so they survive intact
- The cell glyph inside each matrix cell becomes the **dual-layer candle** (price + ghost) rather than the single volume-weighted candle

So the prompt to Cursor needs to be framed as a **substitutional** rebuild, not additive. This is important because if you tell Cursor "add L2 ghost candles to the demo" without being explicit about the axis replacement, it will try to preserve the existing matrix and layer ghosts on top, which is incoherent given your new axes.

## Two honest caveats

**The reversal and continuation detectors become dead weight** until you re-derive their laws for the new axes. The old laws were elegant because Effort×Result maps naturally onto Wyckoff's central analytic framework. Volume×Depth is a different analytic framework (closer to Larry Harris's market microstructure) and its laws are not a simple translation. You'll eventually want laws like "Ghost Divergence" (price color vs. ghost color mismatch) or "Depth Collapse" (ghost width contracting over N bars while price width grows). But those are new designs, not ports. Stubbing them out for now is the right move.

**The 100-cell name table from the Fractal Phatness prototype is uneven in quality.** Some names are evocative and distinct (The Slip, Hidden Floor, Coherence, Wall Build). Others are generic or redundant (Drift, Coil, Steady, Moderate). When you integrate, you may want to curate — keep maybe 25–30 genuinely distinct archetypes and leave the other cells un-named or inherited from their neighbors. But that's a second-pass editorial decision; for the initial Cursor fork, use the full table as-is.
